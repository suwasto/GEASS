## Question

GEASS's entire output is accusation-shaped: it ranks named government agencies by "absurdity" and implies named companies collude. The submission is public, published to GitHub, and shown to judges. Where exactly is the line, and what must the product do to stay behind it?

Module 4 claims human-in-the-loop governance — "GEASS never accuses individuals of crimes autonomously." That is a good sentence in a PRD and a very different thing as an implemented control. Settle which it is before the dossier generator gets built, because it changes what gets generated.

Topics to resolve:

- **Named or anonymised?** Does the deployed public dashboard name real vendors and real agencies at all? The PRD's own screenshots name them. Options range from "full names, it's all public data" through "hashed vendors with an agency-only view" to "everything illustrative." There is a defensible position in each direction; pick one deliberately.
- **What the dossier actually asserts.** The PPID request under UU No. 14/2008 is a *request for information*, not an allegation — legally a very different instrument. Does anything GEASS generates ever assert wrongdoing, or does it only ever request documents and report deltas?
- **A public "absurdity" ranking.** Is a zero-login dashboard that ranks named regional agencies by a model-derived score defensible? What happens when the model is wrong about a named `satker`?
- **Is the HITL governance real?** Concretely: what must a human do before anything publishes? If the answer is "nothing," say so and let the video stop claiming otherwise.
- **The video and the deck.** They will name cases out loud. Those are also published artifacts and are easier to quote than a dashboard.
- **Attribution of the anomaly.** When a unit price exceeds SSH, the gap may be procurement error rather than intent. How does the language keep that distinction?
